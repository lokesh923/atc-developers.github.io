The big picture

Game 3 at a Glance

Key Collection is the third game of the Cognitive (Gamified) Assessment. You move a character across a grid, pick up the key and then walk to the door. The catch: some cells are hidden walls that you only discover when you bump into them.

Difficulty levels you should be ready for

Easy

Small grids (3×3 to 4×4), 1 key and only a few walls. The route is almost direct.

Medium

5×5 to 6×6 grids, 1–2 keys, and walls that force a detour or two.

Hard

8×8 grids and bigger, 2–3 keys, and many walls. Key order and memory matter.

How many levels?The number of Key Collection levels in the test can vary (it may be a single level). This guide and the live practice train you on all three difficulty levels, so whatever you face will feel familiar.

03
Simple but strict

The Rules

1

Move one cell at a time

Up, down, left or right only. No diagonal moves. Click or tap an arrow or a neighbouring cell. Arrow keys may also work.

2

Walls are hidden

You can't see walls in advance. If you try to move into one, you stay where you are and the wall is revealed.

3

Key before door

Walk onto the key to collect it. Reaching the door without the key does nothing.

4

Fewer moves is better

Finish within the timer using as few moves (and bumps) as you can. Efficient routes score better and save time.

04
Count before you move

Manhattan Distance: Your Secret Weapon

With no diagonal moves, the shortest possible number of moves between two cells is:

Manhattan distance|row difference| + |column difference|

Example: from D1 to the key at B4 is 2 rows + 3 columns = 5 moves. Every route without detours has exactly 5 moves, in any order of "right" and "up".

  • Use it to judge your route: if a plan needs far more moves than the Manhattan distance, look for a better one.
  • Walls force detours. Each sidestep around a wall usually adds +2 moves (one out, one back).
  • Any move that reduces the distance to your target is a "good" move. Prefer those.

06
Deal with the unknown

Handling Hidden Walls

You can't avoid every bump, but you can make each bump cheap. Use this order of preference every time you're blocked:

#Try this direction…Why
1The other direction that still gets you closer to the targetNo distance is lost. This was how every wall in Section 5 was handled.
2A sidestep (perpendicular to the target)Costs +2 moves overall, but usually goes around a single wall.
3Back the way you came, to try a different corridorUse this only when you're in a dead end. It costs the most.

🧠 Remember every wall

A revealed wall stays a wall for the whole level. Never test the same blocked move twice. Note it mentally, for example "C1 and C2 are walls, so row C is blocked on the left."

🧭 Read the pattern

Walls often form lines or blocks. Two walls side by side (like C1 and C2) suggest a longer barrier, so look for the gap further along instead of probing every cell.

🚧 Avoid dead-end corridors

If a narrow path leads away from your target, prefer open areas. Corridors are where most costly back-tracking happens.

🛣️ Reuse safe routes

Cells you've already walked through are guaranteed open. When heading to the door, reusing part of a known route is often safer than exploring.

07
Hard levels

Multi-Key Levels: Choosing the Order

With 2–3 keys, the order you collect them in can change your total by many moves. Same grid, same keys, two different orders:

✓ Smart order: 12 moves

Order F3 → D2 → C5: one smooth sweep from the start towards the door.

✗ Zig-zag order: 22 moves

Order C5 → F3 → D2: chasing the "first key you notice" makes you cross the grid back and forth.

How to choose the order in 10 seconds
  1. Picture a line from you to the door.
  2. Collect keys in the order they appear along that line: the closest to you first, the closest to the door last.
  3. A key far off the line? Grab it when you pass nearest to it, not first by default.
08
The complete technique

The 6-Step Method & Pro Strategies

1

Locate

Find yourself, every key and the door before you move.

2

Count

Work out the Manhattan distance for each leg (start → key → door).

3

Order

With several keys, pick a sweep order along the start → door line.

4

Move toward

Each move should reduce the distance to your current target.

5

Re-route smartly

When blocked: other good direction → sidestep → back-track. Remember the wall.

6

Confirm and finish

Check the key is collected (icon gone or counter updated), then go to the door.

Pro tip: the "always closer" ruleIf you're not sure of the best route, simply make every move one that brings you closer to your current target, unless it's blocked. This simple habit is close to perfect on most grids.
