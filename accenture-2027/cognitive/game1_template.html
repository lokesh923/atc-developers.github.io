02
Know the screen

Game 1 Explained: What You'll See

Each question shows three green bubbles in random positions on the screen. Every bubble holds a short arithmetic expression. Your job is to tap them in ascending order of their results: smallest first, largest last.

Reading the screen

  1. Question number (top left): your progress through the 25 questions.
  2. Game label (top right): confirms you're in the Bubbles game.
  3. Instruction: always "Tap the bubbles in ASCENDING order of their result."
  4. Timer: 15 seconds for this question only.
  5. Bubbles: 3 expressions using +, −, × or ÷, placed randomly. Their screen position tells you nothing about the order.

Key facts

What is graded

The order in which you tap the bubbles. You don't type the answers, so you only need the correct ranking.

"Ascending" means

From the smallest result to the largest, like 55 → 60 → 135. It is not left-to-right or top-to-bottom on the screen.

Pace

The next question starts as soon as one ends. Across 25 questions, staying calm and steady matters more than one perfect answer.

Good to knowThis guide describes the 3-bubble version that candidates report. If your screen ever shows a different number of bubbles, the rule stays the same: tap from the smallest result to the largest.
03
Follow along

How to Play, Step by Step

We'll play the sample question shown on the previous page from start to finish.

1Look at all three bubbles

The question appears with three bubbles: 53 + 7, 58 + 77 and 60 − 5. Spend about one second taking in all three before you calculate anything.

Don't start tapping the bubble that happens to be on the left or top.

BubbleQuick estimateExact
58 + 77≈ 60 + 80 = ~140 (clearly the biggest)135
53 + 7≈ 6060
60 − 5a bit less than 6055

2Estimate first, then calculate only if needed

A rough look shows 58 + 77 is far bigger than the other two, so it goes last without any exact maths.

The other two are both near 60, so they are close. Calculate those two exactly: 53 + 7 = 60 and 60 − 5 = 55.

3Tap from the smallest to the largest

The numbered badges show the tap order:

① 60 − 5 (55) → ② 53 + 7 (60) → ③ 58 + 77 (135)

Tap firmly and decisively. Each tap usually counts as final.

4Let it advance

Some versions move on automatically after the third tap, while others need a quick confirm. Either way, don't hesitate once you've decided.

5Reset for the next one

The next question begins immediately. Forget the previous one, whether it was right or wrong, and start again at Step 1.

04
The core technique

The 4-Step Speed Method

Top scorers don't calculate everything. They sort first and calculate only where needed. Use this routine on every question until it becomes automatic.

1

SCAN

About 1 second. Read all three bubbles and notice the operators.

2

ESTIMATE

About 3 seconds. Round the numbers and place each bubble as small, medium or large.

3

VERIFY

About 5 seconds. Calculate exactly only the bubbles whose estimates are close.

4

TAP

About 2 seconds. Tap smallest → middle → largest, firmly.

Why this worksIn most questions, one or two bubbles are obviously bigger or smaller. Estimation sorts those for free, so your limited seconds go to the one close pair that really needs exact maths.
05
Sort without calculating

Operator Instincts & Estimation

×Multiplication
Usually the largest in a mixed set. 9 × 8 = 72 beats most sums of small numbers.
÷Division
Usually the smallest. 36 ÷ 4 = 9: dividing shrinks numbers.
+Addition
Usually in the middle. Fast to work out, so compute it first when needed.
−Subtraction
Usually small to middle. 12 − 9 = 3 can be tiny.
Instinct, not a ruleThese patterns are right most of the time, not always. 2 × 3 = 6 is smaller than 90 ÷ 2 = 45. Always glance at the actual numbers: big numbers can beat any operator.

Rounding to the nearest 10

BubblesRounded estimateOrder from estimates alone
19 + 21, 68 + 7, 4 × 6~40, ~75, ~244×6 → 19+21 → 68+7 ✓ no exact maths needed
91 − 38, 7 × 9, 120 ÷ 4~50, ~63, ~30120÷4 → 91−38 → 7×9 ✓
45 + 18, 8 × 8, 130 ÷ 2~63, 64, 65Too close! Verify exactly: 63, 64, 65

Rule of thumb: if two estimates are more than about 10 apart, trust the estimate. If they are within about 10, calculate both exactly.

06
Calculate faster

Mental Math Toolkit

You have about 5 seconds per bubble. These tricks turn slow "school-style" calculation into quick mental steps.

Addition: round and adjust

Round one number to a friendly 10, add, then fix the difference.

58 + 77 → 60 + 77 = 137, then −2 = 135
49 + 36 → 50 + 36 = 86, then −1 = 85

Addition: tens first, then units

46 + 37 → 40 + 30 = 70, 6 + 7 = 13, 70 + 13 = 83
Work from left to right, the way you read.

Subtraction: count up

Go from the smaller number up to the bigger one.

91 − 38 → 38 → 40 (+2) → 91 (+51) = 53
100 − 19 → 19 → 20 (+1) → 100 (+80) = 81

Subtraction: round the subtracted number

84 − 29 → 84 − 30 = 54, then +1 = 55
73 − 48 → 73 − 50 = 23, then +2 = 25

Multiplication: tables up to 12 × 12

This is non-negotiable. Know them by recall, not by counting.

Hardest ones to drill: 6×7 = 42, 6×8 = 48, 7×8 = 56, 7×9 = 63, 8×9 = 72, 12×7 = 84, 12×8 = 96, 12×9 = 108

Multiplication shortcuts

× 5: × 10 then halve → 14 × 5 = 70
× 9: × 10 then subtract the number → 9 × 13 = 130 − 13 = 117
× 11 (2-digit): 11 × 34 → 3 (3+4) 4 = 374
Split it: 13 × 6 = 10×6 + 3×6 = 60 + 18 = 78

Division: think in reverse

Ask "what times the divisor gives this number?"

84 ÷ 7 → 7 × ? = 84 → 12
144 ÷ 12 → 12 × 12 = 144 → 12

Division shortcuts

÷ 2: halve. ÷ 4: halve twice → 120 ÷ 4 = 60 → 30
÷ 5: double, then ÷ 10 → 85 ÷ 5 = 170 ÷ 10 = 17
Non-whole results are fine: 9 ÷ 2 = 4.5 (between 4 and 5)

Useful number facts to memorise

Squares 11–20

121, 144, 169, 196, 225, 256, 289, 324, 361, 400

Pairs that make 100

63 + 37, 48 + 52, 29 + 71, 85 + 15: spot them instantly

Common products

15×4 = 60, 25×4 = 100, 12×5 = 60, 16×5 = 80, 15×6 = 90

08
Beat the clock

Your 15-Second Time Budget

1s
Estimate · 3s
Verify close pair · 5s
Tap · 2s
Buffer · 4s

Scan → Estimate → Verify → Tap, with about 4 seconds of safety buffer.

SituationWhat to do
Order is obvious from estimatesTap straight away and bank the spare seconds for calm.
Two bubbles are closeCalculate only those two exactly. The third is already placed.
One bubble is hard, like 13 × 7Split it (10×7 + 3×7 = 91), or just bound it: "more than 80, less than 100" is often enough.
About 3 seconds left and still unsureMake your best guess and tap. A guess may be right, but an unanswered question is certainly wrong.
You just got one wrongLet it go immediately. One miss out of 25 is fine, but losing focus for the next 5 is not.
09
Avoid these

Common Mistakes & Traps

Mistake ✗Fix ✓
Tapping bubbles in the order they appear on screenPosition means nothing. Always rank by value.
Calculating all three fully before comparingEstimate first. Calculate exactly only the close ones.
Assuming × is always the biggest and ÷ always the smallestUse instincts as a starting point, then glance at the numbers.
Tapping in descending order by habitRepeat to yourself: "small → big". Ascending means going up.
Freezing on one hard question until the time runs outAt about 3 seconds left, tap your best guess.
Panicking after a wrong answerBreathe out and reset. Every question is a fresh start.
Mixing up − and ÷ under pressureDuring Scan, deliberately notice each operator symbol.
Misjudging values like 4.5 or 7.5A non-whole result simply sits between two whole numbers: 9 ÷ 2 = 4.5 is more than 4 and less than 5.

Get test-ready

7-Day Training Plan

About 30 minutes a day. Mental math speed improves quickly with daily practice, and this one habit has the biggest effect on your score.

DayFocus30-minute routine
Day 1Understand the gameRead Sections 1–4. Play Level 1 with no timer, focusing on the 4-step method.
Day 2Tables15 min of 6–12 times tables (target under 3 s each) and 15 min of 2-digit addition and subtraction.
Day 3TricksPractise every Section 6 trick 10 times each. Redo Level 1 with a 15-second timer.
Day 4EstimationTake random sets of 3 expressions and say the order from estimates only. Then do Level 2 with a timer.
Day 5Close valuesDo Level 3 with a timer. For every miss, write down why: was it a calculation slip or a misread?
Day 6Full simulationDo 25 questions in a row at 15 s each with no breaks, using the live practice tool or Levels 1–3 mixed.
Day 7Light revision10 min of tables, then read Sections 4, 8 and 9. Sleep well, because a fresh brain calculates faster.

Test-Day Checklist

  • Remember: ascending means smallest → largest
  • Scan all three bubbles before you tap anything
  • Estimate first, then verify only the close pair
  • Notice each operator: +, −, × or ÷
  • At about 3 seconds left, tap your best guess
  • Never leave a question untapped
  • Reset immediately after every question
  • Use a mouse or touchpad you're comfortable with, and tap precisely
RememberThis game isn't about being a maths genius. It's about sorting smartly, calculating only what you must, and staying calm for 25 questions. Practise for a week and you'll clear it comfortably.
