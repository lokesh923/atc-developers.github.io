The big picture

Game 2 at a Glance

Pathfinding is the second game in the Cognitive (Gamified) Assessment. A rocket must travel through a grid of track tiles to reach a planet. The track is broken in a few places. Your job is to repair the route by rotating and swapping tiles, then press Check.

Know the screen

The Screen Explained

This illustration is drawn from candidate screenshots. The real grid may be bigger (around 5×5 or more), but every element works the same way.

Question 2 of 3
  1. Question counter (top): which of the 3 puzzles you're on.
  2. Rocket (left edge): the start. It enters the grid at this point.
  3. Planet (right edge): the destination. The route must end here.
  4. Track tiles: each square holds a track piece. The small white arrows show the direction of travel along it.
  5. Row letters and column numbers (A–D, 1–4): we add these in this guide so we can name tiles like C2. The real game doesn't show them.
  6. Timer (bottom left): the countdown for this puzzle.
  7. ⟳ Rotate: turns the selected tile 90°.
  8. ⇄ Swap: exchanges the positions of two tiles.
  9. ✓ Check: submits your route. A correct route shows "Valid route – well done!" with a Continue button.
Versions can differSome candidates describe tapping a tile to cycle its direction, while others see separate Rotate and Swap buttons. The thinking is identical: find where the route breaks, then change that tile. Read the on-screen instructions (and use the practice question, if one is offered) to confirm the controls.
03
The building blocks

Tiles, Arrows & the Connection Rule

Every tile is a piece of track joining two sides of its square. There are two basic shapes:

Straight tile

Joins opposite sides: Left–Right (horizontal) or Up–Down (vertical). The rocket goes straight through.

Corner (turn) tile

Joins two neighbouring sides, like Left–Up or Down–Right. The rocket turns 90°.

The connection rule

A route is valid only when every tile connects to the next The side where the rocket leaves one tile must touch a side where the next tile has track. If a tile sends the rocket right, the tile on its right must have track on its left side. Any gap, wall or wrong-way piece breaks the route.

04
Your two tools

Rotate & Swap: How the Tools Work

⟳ Rotate: turns a tile 90° clockwise each time

One corner tile, rotated 0–3 times. Four taps bring it back to the start, so never tap more than 3 times.

Side now →after 1 tapafter 2 tapsafter 3 taps
UpRightDownLeft
RightDownLeftUp
DownLeftUpRight
LeftUpRightDown

Rotation can fix

A tile that is the right shape but faces the wrong way, for example a corner that turns down when you need it to turn up.

Rotation can't fix

A tile that is the wrong shape. No amount of rotating turns a straight into a corner. That's when you use Swap.

⇄ Swap: exchanges two tiles

Select a tile on the route that has the wrong shape, and swap it with a tile of the right shape from somewhere the rocket never goes. Only swap with tiles that are off the route, or you'll break a part that already works.

05
See it in action

Full Play-Through, Step by Step

Here is one complete puzzle from start to finish. The rocket enters at row C on the left, and the planet is to the right of A4.

1Study the grid before touching anything

Find the start (rocket, entering C1 from the left) and the goal (planet, right of A4).

Don't rotate anything yet. First understand where the rocket would go right now.

2Trace the current route until it breaks

The rocket enters C1 (straight, Left → Right) ✓ and moves into C2.

C2 is a corner that turns down, into D2. But D2 is a horizontal straight with no track on top, so the route breaks at C2 ✗.

We need C2 to turn up towards B2 instead.

3Fix the earliest break: rotate C2 once

C2 joins Left–Down. From the rotation table, 1 tap turns Left→Up and Down→Left, so it now joins Left–Up ✓.

Re-trace from the start: C1 → C2 → B2 (vertical) → A2 (corner, Down → Right) → A3…

A3 is a corner turning down into B3, which has no track on top. It's the next break ✗.

4Wrong shape? Swap it

A3 needs to be a horizontal straight (Left → Right). It's a corner, and rotating a corner can never make a straight.

D3 is a horizontal straight that the rocket never uses, so swap A3 ⇄ D3.

Re-trace: C1 → C2 → B2 → A2 → A3 → A4 → planet ✓

5Verify the whole route, then press ✓ Check

Trace one final time from the rocket to the planet, saying the moves to yourself: "right, up, up, right, right, right".

Only then press ✓ Check. You'll see "Valid route – well done!", then press Continue for the next puzzle.

Total changes: 1 rotation + 1 swap. Most real puzzles need just 1–3 changes like this.

06
The core technique

The 6-Step Solving Method

1

Study

Find the start, the goal and the overall layout. Don't change anything yet.

2

Trace

Follow the current route from the rocket, tile by tile, until it breaks, loops or leaves the grid.

3

Find the earliest break

The first tile where things go wrong is your first fix. Later tiles don't matter yet.

4

Fix with minimum changes

Right shape, wrong direction → Rotate. Wrong shape → Swap with an off-route tile.

5

Re-trace everything

After every change, trace again from the rocket. One early change affects everything after it.

6

Verify, then Check

Press ✓ only when the full route is traced to the planet. Don't submit on a guess.

07
Think like a top scorer

Pro Strategies

🔁 Work backwards from the planet

Ask: "Which tile must deliver the rocket into the planet, and from which side?" Trace backwards a few tiles. Solving from both ends and meeting in the middle is often fastest on long routes.

🥇 Earliest wrong tile first

If the route breaks at tile 3 of 10, fix tile 3 first. The rocket never reaches tiles 4–10 on its current course, so they can't be judged yet.

🌀 Watch for loops

If your trace comes back to a tile it already visited (for example right → down → left → up), the rocket is in a loop. Break the loop by changing one tile in it so it heads towards the planet.

🗣️ Say the directions to yourself

"Right, right, up, up, right…". Saying each move catches tracing slips far better than silently scanning the grid.

🏷️ Name the tiles

Think in coordinates (C1 → C2 → B2…). It stops you losing your place, especially on bigger grids.

↩️ Reset is your friend

If you've made a mess, reset (if the version offers it) and re-trace calmly. Thirty seconds of re-checking beats losing the whole puzzle.
