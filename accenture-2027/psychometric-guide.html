01
The basics

What Is a Psychometric Test?

"Psycho" means mind and "metric" means measurement. A psychometric test measures how you think, behave and react in different situations. It is not about knowledge, so you can't study facts for it the way you would for a technical test.

The behavioural (personality) part asks you to rate statements about yourself, such as "I enjoy working with people from different backgrounds." Your answers build a personality profile. The company then compares that profile with the qualities it wants in its employees.

Why do companies like Accenture use it?

Culture fit

Will you work well in large, diverse, client-facing teams?

Work style

Are you reliable, adaptable and willing to learn new technologies fast?

Scale

It's a fair, quick way to understand thousands of freshers before the interview.

Myths vs Facts

Myth ✗Fact ✓
"There are no right answers, so I can click anything."There are no correct answers, but there are clearly unsuitable ones, such as agreeing that rules can be ignored. Careless clicking also creates inconsistencies.
"I should pick Strongly Agree for every positive statement."An "all extreme" pattern looks unrealistic and can flag your profile as socially desirable responding (trying too hard to look good).
"It's not an elimination round, so it doesn't matter."Candidates report no direct elimination, but your profile may still be used alongside other results. Treat it seriously. It takes only 20 minutes.
"I need to study psychology for this."No. You only need to understand the traits being measured and the question patterns. This guide covers both.
"Neutral is the safest answer."Choosing Neutral too often makes you look indecisive and gives the test little information. Use it rarely.
"Admitting any weakness will hurt me."Small, honest admissions (like feeling nervous before big tasks) make your profile believable. Nobody is perfect, and the test knows that.

03
Recognise the pattern

Question Formats You Will See

The main Accenture format is the agree/disagree statement. It's still worth knowing the other two common formats, because psychometric tests often mix them.

Format 1 · MainSelf-rating statement (Likert scale)

You read a statement about yourself and choose how much you agree.

"I stay calm when deadlines are tight."
Strongly Disagree · Disagree · Neutral · Agree · Strongly Agree

Format 2Situational statement

A workplace situation is described, and you rate how likely you are to react in a given way.

"If a teammate is struggling with their task, I would offer to help even if it's not my responsibility."

Format 3Forced choice (most / least like me)

You see 2–4 statements that all sound positive, and you must pick the one that is most (or least) like you. This makes faking hard, so just pick what is truly closest to you.

A. I plan my work in detail  ·  B. I enjoy meeting new people  ·  C. I like solving difficult problems

The 3-second method
  1. Spot the trait: what quality is this statement about?
  2. Spot the direction: is it positive or negative for a professional?
  3. Pick the strength: use "Strongly" only for core values (integrity, ownership, teamwork). Use plain Agree or Disagree for the rest.
05
What's really being measured

The Traits Being Measured

Most personality tests are built on the Big Five model of personality. Companies then map it to workplace qualities. Learn these once and you will recognise almost every statement.

The Big Five (OCEAN), made simple

TraitWhat it meansWhat a workplace profile usually shows
OpennessCuriosity, open to new ideas and learningHigh: eager to learn new tech and comfortable with change
ConscientiousnessOrganised, responsible, careful, hard-workingHigh: the most valued trait for freshers (meets deadlines, checks work)
ExtraversionSocial energy, talkative, assertiveModerate to high: comfortable with people. You don't need to be the loudest.
AgreeablenessCooperative, kind, helpful, trustingHigh, but balanced: helpful and polite, yet able to disagree respectfully
NeuroticismStress, anxiety, mood swingsLow: calm under pressure, handles criticism well

The 8 workplace traits that matter for IT services

1 · Teamwork & Collaboration

Looks like: shares credit, helps teammates, values other opinions.
Red flag: "I prefer to work alone", "Others slow me down".
"I enjoy working with people who think differently from me." → Agree / Strongly Agree

2 · Adaptability & Flexibility

Looks like: handles changing requirements, new tools, new locations.
Red flag: "I dislike change", "I need a fixed routine".
"I am comfortable when my task changes at short notice." → Agree

3 · Integrity & Ethics

Looks like: honest, follows rules, admits mistakes.
Red flag: any agreement with lying, cheating or hiding errors.
"I would report a mistake even if no one else noticed it." → Strongly Agree

4 · Ownership & Dependability

Looks like: finishes what they start, meets deadlines, takes responsibility.
Red flag: blaming others, leaving tasks half done.
"When I commit to a deadline, I make sure I meet it." → Strongly Agree

5 · Learning Agility

Looks like: enjoys learning, asks questions, picks up new skills fast.
Red flag: "I only like doing what I already know".
"I enjoy learning new technologies on my own." → Strongly Agree

6 · Resilience & Stress Handling

Looks like: stays calm, recovers from failure, accepts feedback.
Red flag: "Criticism upsets me for days", "I panic under pressure".
"I see negative feedback as a chance to improve." → Agree / Strongly Agree

7 · Client & Quality Focus

Looks like: cares about the end user, checks work, pays attention to detail.
Red flag: "Good enough is fine", "Details don't matter".
"I double-check my work before submitting it." → Strongly Agree

8 · Communication & Initiative

Looks like: speaks up, asks for help early, suggests ideas.
Red flag: "I keep problems to myself", "I wait to be told what to do".
"If I am stuck, I ask for help instead of wasting time." → Agree
06
Where most people slip

Consistency Checks & Trap Statements

The test asks about the same trait several times, worded differently and spread across the test. If your answers contradict each other, your profile is marked inconsistent, which is one of the few real ways to perform badly here.

How a consistency cluster works

Here are three statements that all measure teamwork. They might appear as question 5, question 23 and question 47:

QStatementDirectionConsistent answer
5"I enjoy working as part of a team."PositiveSDDNASA
23"I get more done when I work alone than with others."ReversedSDDNASA
47"When my team succeeds, I feel it is my success too."PositiveSDDNASA
The common mistakeA candidate who answers Strongly Agree to Q5 but also Agree to Q23 has contradicted themselves. The test notices this pattern.

Reverse-keyed statements: read every word

A reversed statement describes the opposite of a good trait, so the good answer is Disagree. Watch for these words:

notrarelyavoiddislikefind it difficultprefer to work aloneget upsetput off

"I rarely finish tasks before the deadline."
→ This is about ownership, reversed. Answer Disagree.

"I find it difficult to adjust to new teams."
→ This is about adaptability, reversed. Answer Disagree.

The "too good to be true" trap (lie-scale items)

Some statements describe perfect behaviour that no real human has. They test whether you are answering honestly or just trying to look good. If you strongly agree with all of them, your whole profile may be treated as less believable.

Trap statementSmart answer
"I have never told even a small lie."SDDNASA Disagree
"I always like everyone I meet."SDDNASA Disagree
"I have never felt angry at anyone."SDDNASA Disagree
"I sometimes feel nervous before an important task."SDDNASA Agree is fine, because it's normal
Rule of thumbWords like "never", "always", "everyone" and "in my whole life" next to a perfect behaviour are a signal. Real people are good most of the time, not all of the time.

Remember these

The 12 Golden Rules

1

Be your best professional self

Answer as the person you are at your best at work or college, not on a lazy Sunday.

2

Stay consistent

Decide your "stand" on each trait once, and keep it every time that trait appears.

3

Read every word

"Not", "rarely" and "avoid" flip the meaning. Most mistakes come from skim-reading.

4

Integrity is non-negotiable

Always strongly reject lying, cheating, hiding mistakes or bending rules.

5

Avoid the all-extreme pattern

Mix Agree and Strongly Agree. Save "Strongly" for core values.

6

Limit Neutral

Use it rarely, only when you truly don't lean either way. Aim for fewer than 5 out of 54.

7

Don't claim perfection

Disagree with "I have never…" and "I always…" claims of perfect behaviour.

8

Team over ego

Prefer collaboration, shared credit and helping others, while still owning your own work.

9

Welcome change

Agree with flexibility: new tasks, new tools, relocation, shifting priorities.

10

Show calm, not anxiety

Disagree with panic, stress-outs and taking feedback personally.

11

Trust your first instinct

You get about 22 seconds per question. Overthinking leads to inconsistency.

12

Answer every question

Skipped or timed-out items weaken your profile. Never leave any blank.

Practice set 2

Situational Judgement Practice

These scenarios train you to think like a professional. Even when the real test uses only an agree/disagree scale, the same thinking tells you which statements to agree with. The best answer is highlighted.

1. You notice a bug in code you submitted yesterday. Nobody else has noticed it yet. What do you do?
AStay quiet. If no one finds it, it's not a problem.
BFix it silently without telling anyone.
CInform your lead, explain the impact, and fix it quickly.
DWait to see if testing catches it.
Why C: it shows integrity and ownership. B looks responsible, but hiding a change from the team breaks transparency.
2. A teammate keeps missing their part of the work, which delays the whole team.
AComplain about them to the manager straight away.
BTalk to them privately, understand the issue, and offer help. Escalate only if it continues.
CDo their work yourself without saying anything.
DIgnore it, because it's not your problem.
Why B: it balances empathy, teamwork and responsibility, and keeps escalation as a sensible next step.
3. Your manager assigns you a technology you have never used, with a one-week deadline.
ASay it's not your skill and ask for a different task.
BAccept, make a quick learning plan, use documentation and colleagues, and share progress early.
CAccept and quietly hope you figure it out.
DAccept but ask for the deadline to be doubled right away.
Why B: it shows learning agility, adaptability and communication. Raising a timeline risk after you have a plan is fine.
4. A client asks for a change late on a Friday that could affect the release.
AMake the change immediately without telling anyone.
BRefuse, because it's too late.
CAcknowledge the request politely, check the impact with your lead, and agree a realistic plan with the client.
DReply on Monday.
Why C: it's client-focused but follows process. A risks the release, and B and D ignore the client.
5. Your senior suggests an approach you believe is less efficient.
AFollow it silently. Seniors are always right.
BRespectfully share your alternative with reasons, then go with the team's final decision.
CUse your own approach without telling them.
DArgue until they agree.
Why B: it combines respectful assertiveness with team discipline.
6. You receive harsh feedback on your first project in front of the team.
ADefend yourself strongly in the meeting.
BListen calmly, note the points, ask clarifying questions later, and improve.
CFeel demotivated and avoid that senior.
DIgnore it, because they don't understand your work.
Why B: it shows resilience and a growth mindset.
7. You finish your tasks early. Your team is busy with a deadline.
ALog off early. You've done your part.
BTell your lead you are free and offer to help the team.
CStart a personal side project.
DPretend to be busy.
Why B: it shows initiative, team spirit and transparency.
8. A friend in another team asks you to share confidential client data "just for reference".
AShare it, since they're from the same company.
BShare only part of it.
CPolitely refuse and explain that the data is confidential. Suggest they request access through the proper channel.
DIgnore the message.
Why C: data security and integrity are non-negotiable at an IT services company.
9. You are asked to join a project in a new city at short notice.
ARefuse, because you prefer your current location.
BAccept positively, and discuss any genuine practical concerns with HR or your lead.
CAccept, then look for another job.
DDelay your answer as long as possible.
Why B: it shows adaptability, which matters for a PAN-India role, and honest communication.
10. You are stuck on a problem for two hours and the deadline is close.
AKeep trying alone. Asking makes you look weak.
BSummarise what you've tried and ask a teammate or lead for guidance.
CSubmit incomplete work without saying anything.
DCopy a solution from the internet without understanding it.
Why B: asking early, with context, is professional. It saves time and shows ownership.

Each group below measures one trait with different wording. Answer all items in a group the same way in spirit: agree with the positive ones and disagree with the reversed ones.

Group C · Integrity

aHonesty matters more than results.SDDNASA

Group D · Calm under pressure

Self-check after practice
  • Did you agree with a positive item and its reversed partner? If so, that's an inconsistency. Re-read the reversed item slowly.
  • Did you choose Strongly Agree for more than about 60% of items? If so, mix in plain Agree for non-core traits.
  • Did you pick Neutral more than 4–5 times? If so, make firmer choices.

Your doubts answered

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I fail the behavioural test?

Candidates report no direct elimination from this part. Still, a careless or inconsistent profile is a bad first impression, and it may be used with your other results.

Should I answer 100% honestly?

Answer honestly, from your best professional self. Don't invent a personality, and don't share your worst habits either.

Can I go back and change answers?

Usually not. Treat every answer as final, and read carefully the first time.

Is it okay to choose Neutral?

Yes, occasionally, for statements where you genuinely don't lean either way. Overusing it looks indecisive.

What if two statements seem identical?

That's deliberate: it's a consistency check. Give the same kind of answer, unless one of them is reverse-worded.

I'm an introvert. Is that bad?

No. Many great engineers are introverts. Show that you collaborate and communicate well. You don't need to claim you love parties.

Will the interviewer see my results?

It's possible. That's another reason to answer consistently with how you'll present yourself in the interview.

What happens right after this test?

The Gamified Cognitive test (3 games in 20 min), which is an elimination round. Stay calm and keep your energy for it.
