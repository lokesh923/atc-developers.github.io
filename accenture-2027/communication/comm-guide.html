00
Know the round

Sections, Timing & Scoring

A separate email gives you the login details and the time window. The round is about 30 minutes and is listed as "20 questions · 30 minutes". Those questions are spread over seven speaking/listening sections (A–G) and a compulsory email. It is an elimination round, but it is very trainable.

SectionYou hear / see → you doMain skill scored
A · Listening comprehensionShort audio clip → answer the question in one complete sentenceListening, factual recall, sentence building
B · Conversation responseA short line from a manager, colleague or client → give a polite, professional replyFluency, workplace etiquette
C · Reading aloudRead about 8 sentences aloud at a natural pacePronunciation, pausing at punctuation
D · Listen & repeatRepeat each sentence exactly as you heard itIntonation, stress, auditory memory
E · Fill in the missing wordA sentence with a blank → say the full sentence with the right wordGrammar: agreement, tense, articles
F · Error correctionAn incorrect sentence → say the corrected sentenceGrammar accuracy
G · Speaking on a topic30 s to prepare, then 45 s to speakFluency, vocabulary, structure, confidence
Email (compulsory)Write a professional email of at least 50 wordsEtiquette, structure, grammar, tone
Verbal parameters
Pronunciation · fluency (smooth, steady speech) · active listening · understanding of spoken English · vocabulary · articulation (clear sounds) · grammar.
Written parameters
Email etiquette (greeting, tone, sign-off) · content structure (purpose, details, action) · grammar and spelling accuracy.
How automated scoring "hears" youResponses are usually scored by software. It compares what you said with the expected content, and how you said it (speed, pauses, clarity, stress). It cannot guess unclear words, and long silence is scored as missing content. Clear, steady and complete answers matter more than an accent.
01
Lose no marks to the set-up

Set-up & Test-Day Rules

Before the test
  • Use a headset with a microphone; laptop mics pick up echo and fan noise.
  • A quiet room: fan off, door closed, phone silent.
  • Updated Chrome, stable internet, laptop charged.
  • Allow camera and mic access, and do the sample/mic check.
While speaking
  • Mic about two fingers from your mouth, slightly to the side.
  • Speak at normal conversation volume; do not shout or whisper.
  • Start speaking right after the beep / recording cue.
  • Keep going: if you make a mistake, correct it once and continue.
Rules to respect
  • Video and audio may be recorded for integrity.
  • No one else in the room, no talking to others.
  • Most recordings cannot be redone; there is no pause button.
  • Read every instruction screen. Timers start automatically.
The four biggest score killersSilence of more than 3–4 seconds · one-word answers in sections A and B · speaking too fast (the words blur together) · background noise or a clipped mic. Fix these four and your score rises before you improve anything else.

Sound Clear, Speak Smoothly, Be Correct

These three skills are scored in every spoken section. A few focused fixes make a big difference.

F2
Smooth and natural

Fluency, Pace & Intonation

PaceAbout 120–150 words per minute, calm and steady. If you are nervous, slow down a little; clarity beats speed.
PausesShort pause at a comma, a full stop at a full stop. Pause between word groups, never in the middle of one.
FillersAvoid "umm", "aaa", "like", "basically", "you know". Use a silent 1-second pause instead.
LinkingJoin words smoothly: "turn_it_off", "an_apple". Do not stop after every word.
Intonation (the music of the sentence)
Falling ↘ at the end of statements and wh-questions: "The report is ready ↘." "Where is the meeting ↘?"
Rising ↗ for yes/no questions and lists before the last item: "Are you free today ↗?" "Java ↗, Python ↗ and SQL ↘."
Sentence stress
Stress the content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, numbers, negatives) and keep small words (a, the, to, of, is) light: "I NEED the REPORT by FRIDAY." Stressing the right words is what makes you sound fluent.
10-minute daily drillRecord yourself reading one news paragraph on your phone and play it back. Listen for blurred endings, missing -s/-ed, fillers and rushing. Next, shadow a 1-minute clip: play a sentence and repeat it immediately with the same rhythm.
F3
Rules behind sections E and F

Grammar You Need

Subject–verb agreement

RuleExample
Each / every / either / neither of + plural → singular verbEach of the trainees has a badge.
as well as, along with, with do not change the subjectThe manager, as well as the team, is here.
either…or / neither…nor: the verb follows the nearer subjectNeither Ravi nor his friends were late.
One of + plural noun → singular verbOne of my friends works at TCS.
Uncountable nouns are singularThe information / advice / news / equipment is …
Amounts as one unit are singularFive lakh rupees is a good package.

Articles & prepositions

a / an by soundan hour, an MBA, an honest man · a university, a one-day trip, a European
thesomething specific or unique: the report you sent, the sun, the best, the office
at / on / in (time)at 10 a.m., at night · on Monday, on 5 May · in June, in 2025, in the morning
for / sincefor 3 years (length) · since 2022 (starting point)
Fixed pairsgood at, interested in, depend on, reply to, discuss (no preposition), explain something to someone

Tenses: pick by the time words

Time wordsTenseExample
every day, usually, alwaysPresent simpleShe works in Pune.
now, at the momentPresent continuousI am attending a call.
already, just, yet, ever (no finished time)Present perfectI have already sent it.
since / for + still continuingPresent perfect continuousHe has been working since 9.
yesterday, last week, in 2024, agoPast simpleWe launched it last week.
by the time / before (earlier past)Past perfectThe call had ended before I joined.
tomorrow, next weekwill / going toI will share it tomorrow.
State verbs do not take -ing know, have (possess), want, like, believe, understand, need: "I know him", not "I am knowing him".
Conditionals 1st: If + present, will + verb ("If it rains, I will stay"). 2nd: If + past, would + verb ("If I had time, I would join"). Never put will in the if-clause.
Reported speech moves one tense back: "I am busy" → He said (that) he was busy. Use say something, tell someone.
Indian-English habits the test marks wrongdiscuss about · return back / revert back · cope up with · I am having have experience · Myself I am Rahul · passed out graduated · since two years → for two years · one of my friend friends · senior than to · today morning this morning · do one thing → say the action directly.

THE SEVEN SPOKEN SECTIONS

Section-by-Section Strategy

For each section: what happens, the method, the traps, and examples to practise out loud. Since this is a PDF, the audio is shown as a transcript. Ask a friend to read it to you, or use a text-to-speech app.

A
Listen, then answer in a full sentence

Listening Comprehension

Listen for the 5 Ws→Catch the key fact→Reuse the question's words→One complete sentence
Method
While listening, track who, what, when, where, why, numbers and days. Build your answer from the question: "Why has the meeting been moved?" → "The meeting has been moved because …". Answer only what was asked.
Traps
"Room 4." One-word answers lose most marks. "The meeting is in Room 4." Do not add guesses or extra stories. If you missed a detail, still answer in a full sentence with the part you heard.

B
Reply like a professional

Conversation Response

The 3-part reply
(1) Acknowledge: "Sure", "Of course", "I'm sorry to hear that". (2) Answer or act: what you will do, with a time. (3) Optional polite extra: an offer or a question ("Could you share the agenda?"). Aim for 1–2 complete sentences.
Phrase bank
Agree: "Certainly, I'll …" · Decline politely: "I'm sorry, but … However, I can …" · Clarify: "Could you please tell me …?" · Apologise: "I apologise for the delay; I will …" · Thank: "Thank you, I really appreciate …"
#What you hearA strong reply
1Manager: Can you send me the project report by today?Sure, I will share the project report with you by the end of the day.
2Colleague: I am stuck with this Excel formula. Do you have a minute?Of course. Please share your screen, and we can look at the formula together.
3Client: The application is loading very slowly since yesterday.I am sorry for the inconvenience. I will check the issue right away and update you within an hour.
4Manager: Can you lead the client call tomorrow at 9 a.m.?Yes, I would be happy to lead the call. Could you share the agenda with me today so that I can prepare?
5Colleague: Could you cover my tasks on Friday? I have a family function.I am sorry, but I have a deadline on Friday. However, I can help you with your tasks on Thursday evening.
6Team lead: Why was the deployment delayed last night?The deployment was delayed because two test cases failed at the last minute. We have fixed them, and it is scheduled again for tonight.
7HR: Would you be comfortable relocating to Bengaluru?Yes, I am comfortable relocating to Bengaluru. I see it as a good opportunity to learn and grow.
8Client: Can we move tomorrow's meeting to the afternoon?Certainly. Would 3 p.m. work for you? I will send an updated invitation.

C
Read clearly at a natural pace

Reading Aloud

Method
Use the first second to scan the sentence for difficult words and commas. Read in word groups, pause at punctuation, stress the content words and end statements with a falling tone. Keep a steady pace: do not race through the sentence.
Traps
Skipping or adding small words (a, the, of) · dropping -s and -ed endings · stopping mid-sentence to restart. If you trip over a word, simply continue; restarting costs more than a small slip.

Practise these. Bold = stressed word, | = short pause, || = longer pause.

  1. Our company | has offices in more than forty countries, || and it helps clients | use technology to grow.
  2. Before the meeting starts, | please make sure | that your microphone is muted.
  3. The new software | will be installed on all laptops | by the end of this week.
  4. If you have any questions about the project, | feel free to contact your team lead.
  5. Customer satisfaction | is the most important measure | of our success.
  6. The training programme | covers cloud computing, | data security, | and artificial intelligence.
  7. Although the deadline was tight, || the team delivered the product | on time.
  8. Please remember | to submit your timesheet | every friday | before six p.m.
D
Exactly what you heard

Listen & Repeat

Method
Listen for the meaning, not single words; a sentence you understand is easy to rebuild. Mentally split it into 2–3 chunks ("The training session | will start | after the coffee break"). Copy the speaker's rhythm and falling tone. Start right after the beep.
Traps
Changing words ("will start" → "starts") · dropping articles and plural -s · long silence while trying to remember. Saying 90% of the sentence fluently scores better than silence.

Practise in three lengths: ask someone to read each sentence once, then repeat it.

Short (4–5+ words)
  1. Please close the door.
  2. The report is ready.
  3. I will call you later.
  4. Lunch is served at one.
Medium (6–6+ words)
  1. The manager approved my leave request.
  2. Our team works on a banking project.
  3. Could you send me the meeting link?
  4. The client visit has been postponed.
Long (9–12+ words)
  1. The training session will start after the coffee break.
  2. Most employees prefer working from the office on Mondays.
  3. Please update the document before you share it with the client.
  4. Our new office in Pune has a large cafeteria and a gym.
E
Grammar in your voice

Fill in the Missing Word

Method
Find the clue words around the blank: a time word (since, yesterday), a subject (each of, neither…nor), the next word's sound (a/an) or a fixed pair (good ___ coding). Then say the whole sentence with the word in it, smoothly.
Traps
Saying only the missing word · changing other words in the sentence · forgetting the article in the full sentence. Revise F3: most items test agreement, tense, articles and prepositions.

F
Spot it, fix it, say it

Error Correction

Method
Check, in this order: (1) subject–verb agreement, (2) tense versus the time words, (3) articles and singular/plural, (4) prepositions and extra words (discuss about), (5) word form (senior than to). Then say the full corrected sentence.
Traps
Fix only the error and keep the rest of the sentence the same. Rewriting the whole sentence can introduce new mistakes. Say the corrected sentence, not an explanation.

G
30 s to prepare, 45 s to speak

Speaking on a Topic

Opening: your view in one line→Point 1 + reason / example→Point 2 + reason / example→Closing: sum up in one line
Use the 30 seconds
Write 3 keywords only: your view, point 1, point 2. Do not write sentences. Think of one real example from college, projects or internships.
Linkers that sound fluent
In my opinion · Firstly · For example · Moreover · On the other hand · However · That is why · In conclusion / To sum up.
Timing
45 seconds is about 90–100 words at a calm pace. Keep speaking until the timer ends. If you finish early, add one more example rather than stopping.
G1Work from home or work from office: which is better?91 words ≈ 45 s
30-second prep notes
  • View: a hybrid model works best
  • Home: saves travel time, fewer distractions
  • Office: teamwork, faster learning for freshers

In my opinion, a hybrid model is the best option. Working from home saves a lot of travel time and helps people focus on tasks that need deep concentration. However, the office has its own advantages. When we sit together, we solve problems faster, and freshers learn much more by watching experienced colleagues. Informal conversations also build trust in a team. So, I believe two or three days in the office and the rest at home gives us the best of both worlds. It keeps productivity high and the team connected.

G2The role of technology in education87 words ≈ 45 s
30-second prep notes
  • View: technology has made learning open to everyone
  • Online courses and videos for any topic
  • Needs balance: screen time and discipline

Technology has completely changed the way we learn. Today, a student in a small town can watch lectures from the best teachers in the world, often for free. Online courses, videos and practice platforms let us learn at our own pace and revise whenever we want. During my own preparation, I used coding websites to practise every day. However, technology also brings distractions, so self-discipline is very important. In conclusion, technology is a powerful tool for education, as long as we use it with a clear goal.

G3Your biggest strength89 words ≈ 45 s
30-second prep notes
  • Strength: I learn new things quickly
  • Example: learned React in two weeks for a college project
  • Why it matters in IT

My biggest strength is that I learn new things quickly. For example, in my final year, our team needed a web interface for our project, but none of us knew React. I took the responsibility, followed online tutorials and built the first version within two weeks. Later, I also helped my teammates understand the code. In the IT industry, technologies change very fast, so the ability to learn quickly is extremely valuable. I believe this strength will help me adapt to any project and contribute from the first day.

G4Why is teamwork important?92 words ≈ 45 s
30-second prep notes
  • View: big goals need many skills
  • Example: college hackathon
  • Teams share load and ideas

Teamwork is important because most real projects are too big for one person. A software product needs developers, testers, designers and business analysts working together. When people with different skills share ideas, the final result is much better. I experienced this during a college hackathon, where our team of four built a working app in twenty-four hours. We divided the work according to our strengths and supported each other when someone was stuck. Good teamwork also reduces stress, because the workload is shared. That is why companies value team players so much.

G5Social media: a boon or a bane?94 words ≈ 45 s
30-second prep notes
  • View: both, depends on how we use it
  • Boon: connection, learning, opportunities
  • Bane: addiction, fake news

I think social media is both a boon and a bane, and it depends on how we use it. On the positive side, it connects people across the world, helps small businesses reach customers and gives us access to useful information. Platforms like LinkedIn also help students find internships and jobs. On the negative side, too much social media can become addictive, waste our time and spread fake news very quickly. So, the key is balance. If we use it with purpose and check facts before sharing, social media can be a great tool.

G6How do you handle pressure?100 words ≈ 45 s
30-second prep notes
  • Stay calm and plan
  • Example: two deadlines in one week
  • Break work into small tasks, communicate early

I handle pressure by staying calm and making a clear plan. When I have too much work, I first list all the tasks and decide which ones are most urgent. Then I break big tasks into smaller steps, so that I can see progress. For example, last semester I had two project submissions and an exam in the same week. I made a day-by-day schedule, finished the coding part first and asked my professor early about one doubt. I completed everything on time. I also believe in communicating early if I need help, instead of waiting until the last moment.

G7A skill you want to learn93 words ≈ 45 s
30-second prep notes
  • Skill: cloud computing
  • Why: most companies are moving to the cloud
  • Plan: certification + projects

One skill I really want to learn is cloud computing. Today, most companies are moving their applications and data to cloud platforms like AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, so cloud skills are in very high demand. I have already studied the basics, such as virtual machines and storage, and now I plan to complete a beginner certification. I also want to deploy one of my college projects on the cloud, because practical experience is more valuable than theory. I believe this skill will make me a stronger engineer and open many career opportunities.

G8The importance of time management93 words ≈ 45 s
30-second prep notes
  • View: time is the one resource we cannot get back
  • Plan the day, prioritise
  • Example: placement preparation

Time management is important because time is the one resource we can never get back. People who manage their time well finish their work on time, feel less stressed and still have time for family and health. A simple method is to plan the day in the morning and do the most important task first. During my placement preparation, I divided my day into fixed slots for aptitude, coding and communication practice. This routine helped me stay consistent. In short, good time management is the foundation of success in both studies and work.

WRITTEN TASK · COMPULSORY

Email Writing

A short professional email of at least 50 words. Use the same structure every time, so the whole email takes under 5 minutes.

M1
The formula

Structure, Tone & Phrase Bank

Subject line→Greeting→Purpose (1 line)→Details / reason→Action / assurance→Polite close→Sign-off + name
PartPhrases you can use
SubjectShort and specific: "Leave Request for 14–15 November" · "Update on the Payment Module" · "Apology for Missing Today's Meeting"
GreetingDear Mr. / Ms. Surname, (formal) · Dear Team, · Hi Arjun, (a friendly colleague only)
PurposeI am writing to … · I would like to request … · This is to inform you that … · Thank you for …
DetailsDue to … · Since this morning … · During testing, we found … · As discussed in yesterday's meeting …
Action / assuranceI will … by (date). · I have already … · Rohit will handle … in my absence. · This will not happen again.
ClosePlease let me know if you have any questions. · I would be grateful if … · Thank you for your understanding / support.
Sign-offBest regards, · Kind regards, · Regards, · Thank you, then your full name on the next line
Do
Answer every point in the task · keep 3 short paragraphs · polite, positive tone · specific dates and times · 60–100 words · check spelling, capital letters and full stops.
Do not
SMS language (u, pls, plz, thx) · ALL CAPS · emojis · "Respected Sir/Madam" chains · very long sentences · blaming others · forgetting the subject line or your name.

Six Model Emails

E1You missed yesterday's team meeting. Write an email to your manager, Mr. Sharma, apologising and explaining.69 words
Subject: Apology for Missing Yesterday's Team Meeting

Dear Mr. Sharma,

I am writing to apologise for missing yesterday's team meeting. Due to an unexpected network outage at my location, I was unable to join the call.

I have gone through the minutes of the meeting and noted the action items assigned to me. I will complete them by Thursday. I have also arranged a backup internet connection so that this does not happen again.

Thank you for your understanding.

Best regards,
Ankit Verma

7-Day Plan & Final Cheat Sheet

DayPlan (40–60 minutes, out loud)
Day 1Set up the headset. Record yourself reading section C; fix pace and pauses. Learn F1 (sounds and stress).
Day 2Grammar F3. Do all fill-in items in section E aloud, then check the answer key.
Day 3Error correction (section F) aloud. Note every rule you missed. Revise the Indian-English habits list.
Day 4Listening (A) and conversation (B): a friend reads the transcripts, you answer in full sentences.
Day 5Listen and repeat (D) with shadowing. Speaking (G): 3 topics with a 30 + 45 second timer.
Day 6Write 3 emails from the M2 tasks without looking, then compare. 5 more G topics.
Day 7Full run-through of all sections in one sitting with a timer. Read the cheat sheet. Test the set-up again.

Speaking

  • Headset, quiet room, mic two fingers away
  • Start right after the beep, no long silence
  • 120–150 words per minute: calm and steady
  • Pause at commas and full stops
  • Stress content words; falling tone at the end
  • Pronounce final -s and -ed
  • No fillers: a silent pause instead
  • A mistake? Correct once and continue

Sections

  • A: one full sentence, reuse the question's words
  • B: acknowledge + action + polite extra
  • C: read in word groups, don't restart
  • D: remember the meaning, repeat in chunks
  • E: say the whole sentence, not one word
  • F: fix only the error, say the full sentence
  • G: view → 2 points → conclusion, 45 s

Email & grammar

  • Subject · greeting · purpose · details · action · close · name
  • At least 50 words (aim for 60–100)
  • Each of / one of → singular verb
  • since + point, for + duration
  • Finished time → past simple
  • discuss, return, cope with: no extra words
  • Tell someone, say something
  • No SMS language, no ALL CAPS
