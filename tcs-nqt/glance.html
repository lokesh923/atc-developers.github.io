Numerical Ability

20Questions

15 of 20Target correct

Official syllabus: Number System | LCM & HCF | Percentages | Profit & Loss | Time, Speed & Distance | Time & Work | SI & CI | Averages | Ratio & Proportion | Probability | P & C | Mensuration | Ages | Mixtures

Topics and what to master

#TopicPriorityWhat to master
1PercentagesMust-doFraction-to-percentage table, successive change, population and salary problems. Convert to fractions to calculate faster.
2Profit & LossMust-doCP, SP, MP, discount, successive discounts and false weights. Always take the percentage on the right base.
3Time, Speed & DistanceMust-doAverage and relative speed, trains, boats and streams, races, meeting and chasing problems. Keep units consistent.
4Time & WorkMust-doEfficiency and total-work method, pipes and cisterns, alternate days, work and wages.
5Ratio & ProportionMust-doDividing amounts, compound ratio, direct and inverse proportion, partnership basics.
6Number SystemMust-doDivisibility, remainders, unit digits, number of factors, base conversion and trailing zeros.
7AveragesImportantWeighted average, change in average on adding or removing a value, age-based averages.
8LCM & HCFImportantPrime factorisation, bells ringing together, greatest number with a given remainder.
9SI & CIImportantSI and CI formulas, half-yearly and quarterly, difference between CI and SI for 2 and 3 years.
10ProbabilityImportantCoins, dice, cards and bags; at least and at most; independent events.
11Permutation & CombinationImportantnPr, nCr, arrangements with repetition, word rank, restricted selection.
12MensurationImportantArea and perimeter of 2D shapes; volume and surface area of cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone and sphere.
13Mixtures & AlligationImportantAlligation rule, replacement of liquid, cost of mixtures.
14Ages, Simplification, ApproximationGood to knowAge equations and ratios, BODMAS, squares and cubes, quick approximation.

Must-know rules

  • Learn squares to 30, cubes to 15, tables to 20 and common fractions as percentages (1/7 = 14.28%).
  • Successive percentage change: a + b + ab/100.
  • Speed in km/h to m/s: multiply by 5/18. Reverse: multiply by 18/5.
  • Work done = efficiency x time. Take LCM of days as total work.
  • Profit% = (SP − CP)/CP x 100, always on cost price unless stated.

Common traps

  • Taking percentage on the wrong base (CP vs SP, old vs new value).
  • Forgetting to convert minutes to hours in speed questions.
  • Spending over 2 minutes on one question. Skip and return.
  • Rounding too early in TITA (typed answer) questions.
  • Leaving questions blank. There is no negative marking.

Official syllabus: Series | Coding – Decoding | Blood Relations | Directions | Seating Arrangements | Syllogisms | Analogies | Odd One Out | Clock & Calendar | Ranking | Alphabet Test | Symbol Operations

Topics and what to master

#TopicPriorityWhat to master
1Series (number and alphabet)Must-doArithmetic, geometric, square, cube, alternating and mixed series; missing and wrong term; letter positions.
2Coding – DecodingMust-doLetter shifts, reverse alphabet, number and symbol codes, conditional and mixed coding.
3Seating ArrangementsMust-doLinear (one and two rows), circular and square tables. Draw the layout first and place definite clues before conditional ones.
4SyllogismsMust-doAll, Some, No and Some-not; Venn diagrams; definite vs possibility; either-or conclusions.
5Blood RelationsMust-doFamily trees with gender marks, coded relations, pointing to a photograph.
6Directions & DistancesImportantNet displacement with Pythagoras, left and right turns, shadow-based directions.
7Clock & CalendarImportantAngle between hands, overlaps, gain and loss, day of the week using odd days, leap years.
8Ranking & OrderingImportantPosition from left and right, total persons = rank from top + rank from bottom − 1, interchange of positions.
9Alphabet Test & Symbol OperationsImportantAlphabet positions, meaningful words, letter counting, redefined operators and coded inequalities.
10Analogies & Odd One OutGood to knowWord, number and letter analogies; classification. Quick marks if you know the patterns.

Must-know rules

  • Positions: A=1 … Z=26, and opposite letter pairs add to 27.
  • Total persons in a row = position from left + position from right − 1.
  • Venn diagram for every syllogism, never intuition.
  • Clock: angle = |30H − 5.5M|. Hands overlap 11 times in 12 hours.
  • Odd days: ordinary year 1, leap year 2, century 0 or 1 (400 years = 0).

Common traps

  • Mixing left and right in direction and seating questions.
  • Treating 'some' as 'all' in syllogisms.
  • Starting a puzzle set without a table or diagram.
  • Missing the word 'definitely' in conclusions.
  • Forgetting that facing direction flips left and right in circular seating.

Official syllabus: Sentence Completion (20 items, 25 s each) | Passage Recall (4 + 4, 30 s read + 90 s write) | Email Writing (1, 9 min) | Instruction screens

Topics and what to master

#TopicPriorityWhat to master
1Sentence Completion: grammarMust-doTenses, subject-verb agreement, articles, prepositions, pronouns, conjunctions. Eliminate wrong options in seconds.
2Passage RecallMust-doThe passage disappears after 30 seconds. Note names, numbers and order of events, then rebuild in short clear sentences within 90 seconds.
3Email WritingMust-doSubject, greeting, purpose, details, action, closing. Practise request, complaint, apology and follow-up emails in 9 minutes.
4Sentence Completion: vocabularyImportantSynonyms, antonyms, word choice and collocations. Learn 10 words daily.
5Idioms, Phrases & UsageImportantCommon idioms, phrasal verbs, confusing word pairs.
6Error Spotting & ParallelismImportantSentence structure, modifiers, parallel construction.
7Typing Speed & AccuracyImportantRecall and email are typed. Aim for 30+ words per minute with few errors.
8Instruction ScreensGood to knowShort timed screens (25 s and 15 s). Read fast; the timer keeps running.

Must-know rules

  • Each item has its own timer: 25 s for each sentence, 120 s for each recall task, 540 s for the email.
  • Subject-verb agreement: find the true subject, ignore words in between.
  • Email structure: Subject → Greeting → Purpose → Details → Action → Closing.
  • In recall, write facts (who, what, when, number) first, then connect them.
  • Check spelling and punctuation before the email timer ends.

Common traps

  • Spending 40 s on one sentence-completion item.
  • Trying to memorise the passage word for word.
  • Writing a long email and running out of time.
  • Informal tone or text-style spelling in emails.
  • Slow typing that wastes the 90 s recall window.

Official syllabus: Data Interpretation | Set Theory & Venn Diagrams | Data Sufficiency | Syllogisms | Advanced Probability | Permutation & Combination | Partnership | Critical Reasoning | Analytical Puzzles

Topics and what to master

#TopicPriorityWhat to master
1Data InterpretationMust-doTables, bar, line, pie and mixed charts. Percentage change, share of total, ratios and averages. Approximate to save time.
2Analytical PuzzlesMust-doMulti-variable arrangement, scheduling, truth-and-liar and grouping puzzles. Build a table first.
3Data SufficiencyMust-doCheck Statement I alone, then II alone, then both together. Decide sufficiency, do not solve fully.
4Permutation & Combination (Adv.)Must-doRestricted arrangements, distribution of objects, circular and digit-formation problems.
5Set Theory & Venn DiagramsImportantTwo and three sets, only/exactly/at least counts, maximum and minimum overlap.
6Advanced ProbabilityImportantConditional probability, with and without replacement, arrangement-based probability.
7PartnershipImportantInvestment x time, entry and exit of partners, working partner with salary.
8Critical ReasoningImportantAssumption, inference, strengthen and weaken, course of action.
9Syllogisms (Advanced)Good to know3–4 statement sets with possibility cases, solved with Venn diagrams.

Must-know rules

  • Two sets: n(A∪B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A∩B).
  • Three sets: add singles, subtract pairs, add the triple intersection.
  • Data sufficiency order: I alone → II alone → both → neither.
  • Approximate in DI: 48.7% of 623 ≈ 50% of 620.
  • Start with the question group that has the most questions (a DI set or puzzle).

Common traps

  • Solving the whole DI set before reading the questions.
  • Calculating exactly when an approximation is enough.
  • Getting stuck on one puzzle for over 4 minutes.
  • Using both statements before testing each alone.
  • Chasing all 14 questions instead of securing 9–10.

Official syllabus: Languages: C | C++ | Java | Python | Perl. Problem types: arrays | strings | math | sorting | recursion | DP and more. Scoring is by hidden test cases.

Topics and what to master

#TopicPriorityWhat to master
1ArraysMust-doTraversal, rotation, prefix sums, Kadane, duplicates and missing numbers, rearrangement, frequency counting.
2StringsMust-doPalindromes, anagrams, frequency, substrings, compression, reverse words, pattern matching.
3Math & Number TheoryMust-doPrime and sieve, GCD and LCM, Fibonacci, digit problems, modular arithmetic, base conversion.
4Sorting & SearchingMust-doCore sorts, binary search and its variants, custom comparators, k-th element.
5Programming FundamentalsMust-doInput and output formats, multiple test cases, loops, patterns, data types and overflow.
6Hashing, Sets & MapsMust-doTwo-sum, group anagrams, first non-repeating element, subarray sum equals K.
7Greedy & Dynamic ProgrammingMust-doCoin change, knapsack, LIS, LCS, grid paths, subset sum. Usually the Medium problem.
8Two Pointers & Sliding WindowImportantPair sum, longest unique substring, fixed and variable windows.
9Recursion & BacktrackingImportantSubsets, permutations, N-Queens, combination sum, maze paths.
10Stack & QueueImportantBalanced parentheses, next greater element, expression evaluation.
11Matrix / 2D ArraysImportantSpiral and diagonal traversal, rotation, transpose, search in sorted matrix.
12Simulation & Real-world ProblemsImportantDate and time, billing and parking logic, grid movement, scheduling.
13Linked List, Trees & GraphsGood to knowReverse list, cycle detection, tree traversals, BFS and DFS basics.
14Bit ManipulationGood to knowSet bits, power of two, XOR tricks, toggling bits.

Must-know rules

  • n up to 10^5 needs about O(n log n) or better. n up to 10^3 can use O(n²).
  • Write a brute-force solution first so you have partial marks, then optimise.
  • Use 64-bit integers (long long, long) when sums or products can be large.
  • Always test: empty input, one element, all equal, maximum size, negatives.
  • Solve the Easy problem fully in about 35 minutes before the Medium.

Common traps

  • Not reading constraints before choosing the approach.
  • Missing multiple test cases or a wrong input and output format.
  • Integer overflow and off-by-one errors.
  • Spending 60 minutes on the Easy problem.
  • Switching languages during preparation.
