How to use this roadmap

Four phases, one goal: a first-attempt TCS NQT clear. Follow the order, do not skip the checkpoints.

🎯 Target

Foundation: clear every section comfortably. Advanced: 9–10 right in Quant + Reasoning and 2 coding problems (at least the Easy fully).

⏱ Daily time

Weekdays 3 hours, weekends 5 hours. With less time, extend the plan rather than skipping sections.

📓 Two notebooks

A formula notebook and an error log. Every wrong question goes in the error log with the reason.
PHASE 1

Foundation

Days 1–30
Build the base. Learn every basic concept, formulas and a coding language, with no pressure on speed yet.
  • Finish all basic Numerical and Reasoning topics
  • Pick ONE coding language (C++, Java or Python) and master the basics
  • Start a daily 15-minute grammar and vocabulary habit
  • Make a formula notebook and an error log from day 1
★ Checkpoint Test 1 on Day 30
PHASE 2

Build

Days 31–60
Go deeper. Cover the rest of the syllabus, including the Advanced Quant and Reasoning topics and intermediate coding.
  • Complete the remaining aptitude topics and all DI and puzzle sets
  • Cover sorting, hashing, recursion, stack and queue in code
  • Start Passage Recall and Email Writing practice
  • Move from untimed to timed practice
★ Checkpoint Test 2 on Day 60
PHASE 3

Sharpen

Days 61–80
Speed and accuracy. Section-wise timed tests, Medium coding problems, and a weekly full mock.
  • Solve Medium coding problems with DP, greedy and graphs basics
  • Do sectional tests under the exact TCS timers
  • Attempt 1 full mock per week, then 2
  • Fix weak topics from your error log
★ Full mocks on Days 70, 75 and 80
PHASE 4

Final Sprint

Days 81–90
Exam simulation. Full-length 190-minute mocks, revision of formulas and templates, and exam-day readiness.
  • Full 190-minute mocks every second day
  • Revise formula notebook, error log and code templates
  • Light practice only in the last 2 days
  • Prepare documents, device, internet and sleep schedule
★ Final mock on Day 88

Topics and what to master

3Time, Speed & DistanceMust-doAverage and relative speed, trains, boats and streams, races, meeting and chasing problems. Keep units consistent.
4Time & WorkMust-doEfficiency and total-work method, pipes and cisterns, alternate days, work and wages.
5Ratio & ProportionMust-doDividing amounts, compound ratio, direct and inverse proportion, partnership basics.
6Number SystemMust-doDivisibility, remainders, unit digits, number of factors, base conversion and trailing zeros.
7AveragesImportantWeighted average, change in average on adding or removing a value, age-based averages.
8LCM & HCFImportantPrime factorisation, bells ringing together, greatest number with a given remainder.
9SI & CIImportantSI and CI formulas, half-yearly and quarterly, difference between CI and SI for 2 and 3 years.
10ProbabilityImportantCoins, dice, cards and bags; at least and at most; independent events.
11Permutation & CombinationImportantnPr, nCr, arrangements with repetition, word rank, restricted selection.
12MensurationImportantArea and perimeter of 2D shapes; volume and surface area of cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone and sphere.
13Mixtures & AlligationImportantAlligation rule, replacement of liquid, cost of mixtures.
14Ages, Simplification, ApproximationGood to knowAge equations and ratios, BODMAS, squares and cubes, quick approximation.

Must-know rules

  • Learn squares to 30, cubes to 15, tables to 20 and common fractions as percentages (1/7 = 14.28%).
  • Successive percentage change: a + b + ab/100.
  • Speed in km/h to m/s: multiply by 5/18. Reverse: multiply by 18/5.
  • Work done = efficiency x time. Take LCM of days as total work.
  • Profit% = (SP − CP)/CP x 100, always on cost price unless stated.

Common traps

  • Taking percentage on the wrong base (CP vs SP, old vs new value).
  • Forgetting to convert minutes to hours in speed questions.
  • Spending over 2 minutes on one question. Skip and return.
  • Rounding too early in TITA (typed answer) questions.
  • Leaving questions blank. There is no negative marking.

Numerical Ability: 90-day strategy

Day-by-day plan, exam tactics and score milestones for this section.

Numerical Ability20 Qs · 25 min · ~75 s each

Accuracy first, speed second. Many questions are formula-based, so recall speed wins marks.

90-day plan for this section
  • Days 1–30: learn concepts, write formulas in a notebook, solve 15–20 questions daily by topic.
  • Days 31–60: timed topic sets (10 questions in 12 minutes), practise TITA style (type the exact answer).
  • Days 61–90: sectional tests of 20 questions in 25 minutes, then review every wrong answer.
Strategy and exam tactics
  • Do the easy question types first (percentages, averages, profit and loss, ratio). Leave long calculations for the end.
  • Skip any question that takes over 2 minutes and mark it for return. Question switching is allowed inside a section.
  • No negative marking, so never leave a blank. Make an informed guess in the last minute.
  • Learn tables up to 20, squares up to 30, cubes up to 15 and common fractions to percentages.
Common mistakes: Spending 4 minutes on one hard problem · Calculation errors under pressure · Ignoring units and rounding in TITA questions

Learning order

Percentages → Profit & Loss → Ratio and Averages → TSD → Time & Work → Number System → LCM/HCF → SI & CI → P&C and Probability → Mensuration → Ages and Mixtures

Daily target

20 new questions + 10 revision questions daily. Weekly: 1 sectional test of 20 Qs in 25 min.

Self-check questions

  • How do I find the unit digit of a large power quickly?
  • Can I convert any fraction to a percentage in 5 seconds?
  • Do I know relative speed formulas for trains and boats?
  • Can I solve a 3-person work problem using efficiency?

Must-know rules

  • Positions: A=1 … Z=26, and opposite letter pairs add to 27.
  • Total persons in a row = position from left + position from right − 1.
  • Venn diagram for every syllogism, never intuition.
  • Clock: angle = |30H − 5.5M|. Hands overlap 11 times in 12 hours.
  • Odd days: ordinary year 1, leap year 2, century 0 or 1 (400 years = 0).

Common traps

  • Mixing left and right in direction and seating questions.
  • Treating 'some' as 'all' in syllogisms.
  • Starting a puzzle set without a table or diagram.
  • Missing the word 'definitely' in conclusions.
  • Forgetting that facing direction flips left and right in circular seating.

Reasoning Ability: 90-day strategy

Day-by-day plan, exam tactics and score milestones for this section.

Reasoning Ability20 Qs · 25 min · ~75 s each

Pattern recognition and clean diagrams. Practice makes this the most scoring Foundation section.

90-day plan for this section
  • Days 1–30: series, coding-decoding, blood relations, directions, seating basics.
  • Days 31–60: puzzles, syllogisms with Venn diagrams, clock and calendar, inequality.
  • Days 61–90: timed sets of 20 in 25 minutes. Aim for 85%+ accuracy.
Strategy and exam tactics
  • For puzzle sets, draw a table first. One set gives 3–5 questions, so it is worth the setup time.
  • Solve series and coding-decoding in under 40 seconds each. They are the quickest marks.
  • For syllogisms, use Venn diagrams every time, never intuition.
  • Read all conditions before placing anything in a seating arrangement.
Common mistakes: Rushing the setup of a puzzle · Mixing left and right in direction questions · Missing the word 'definitely' in syllogism conclusions

Learning order

Series → Coding-Decoding → Blood Relations → Directions → Seating → Syllogisms → Clock & Calendar → Ranking → Alphabet Test → Symbol Operations → Analogies

Daily target

15 questions + 1 puzzle set daily. Weekly: 1 sectional test of 20 Qs in 25 min.

Self-check questions

  • Can I draw a Venn diagram for any syllogism in 30 seconds?
  • Can I find the day of any date quickly using odd days?
  • Can I set up a circular seating table without errors?
  • Can I decode letter shifts without writing every letter?

Must-know rules

  • Each item has its own timer: 25 s for each sentence, 120 s for each recall task, 540 s for the email.
  • Subject-verb agreement: find the true subject, ignore words in between.
  • Email structure: Subject → Greeting → Purpose → Details → Action → Closing.
  • In recall, write facts (who, what, when, number) first, then connect them.
  • Check spelling and punctuation before the email timer ends.

Common traps

  • Spending 40 s on one sentence-completion item.
  • Trying to memorise the passage word for word.
  • Writing a long email and running out of time.
  • Informal tone or text-style spelling in emails.
  • Slow typing that wastes the 90 s recall window.

Verbal Ability: 90-day strategy

Day-by-day plan, exam tactics and score milestones for this section.

Verbal Ability (2027 pattern)25 + tasks · 25 min · timed per item

Three task types with hard per-question timers: Sentence Completion, Passage Recall and Email Writing.

90-day plan for this section
  • Days 1–30: grammar rules (tenses, agreement, articles, prepositions) and 10 new words daily.
  • Days 31–60: idioms and phrases, Passage Recall practice (30 s read, 90 s write) and email formats.
  • Days 61–90: practise the full flow with timers: 20 sentences at 25 s each, 4+4 recall tasks, one 9-minute email.
Strategy and exam tactics
  • Sentence Completion: eliminate grammatically wrong options first. 25 seconds means no time to re-read twice.
  • Passage Recall: note names, numbers and the order of events. Reconstruct short, clear sentences.
  • Email Writing: use a fixed structure: subject, greeting, purpose, details, action, closing. Keep it under 150 words and check spelling.
  • Improve typing speed. Even a good memory fails if typing is slow.
Common mistakes: Writing long emails and running out of time · Grammar errors in the email · Not practising typing

Learning order

Tenses and agreement → Articles and prepositions → Vocabulary and idioms → Sentence completion speed → Passage Recall → Email Writing → Full flow with timers

Daily target

20 sentence-completion questions, 1 recall task and 1 email (from week 6) daily.

Self-check questions

  • Can I choose the right tense in under 10 seconds?
  • Can I recall names, numbers and order from a 30 s passage?
  • Do I have 4 email templates (request, complaint, apology, follow-up)?
  • Is my typing speed above 30 words per minute?

Topics and what to master

2Analytical PuzzlesMust-doMulti-variable arrangement, scheduling, truth-and-liar and grouping puzzles. Build a table first.

4Permutation & Combination (Adv.)Must-doRestricted arrangements, distribution of objects, circular and digit-formation problems.
5Set Theory & Venn DiagramsImportantTwo and three sets, only/exactly/at least counts, maximum and minimum overlap.
6Advanced ProbabilityImportantConditional probability, with and without replacement, arrangement-based probability.
7PartnershipImportantInvestment x time, entry and exit of partners, working partner with salary.
8Critical ReasoningImportantAssumption, inference, strengthen and weaken, course of action.
9Syllogisms (Advanced)Good to know3–4 statement sets with possibility cases, solved with Venn diagrams.

Must-know rules

  • Two sets: n(A∪B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A∩B).
  • Three sets: add singles, subtract pairs, add the triple intersection.
  • Data sufficiency order: I alone → II alone → both → neither.
  • Approximate in DI: 48.7% of 623 ≈ 50% of 620.
  • Start with the question group that has the most questions (a DI set or puzzle).

Common traps

  • Solving the whole DI set before reading the questions.
  • Calculating exactly when an approximation is enough.
  • Getting stuck on one puzzle for over 4 minutes.
  • Using both statements before testing each alone.
  • Chasing all 14 questions instead of securing 9–10.

Advanced Quant + Reasoning: 90-day strategy

Day-by-day plan, exam tactics and score milestones for this section.

Advanced Quant + Reasoning14 Qs · 25 min · ~107 s each

Harder and multi-step. Smart selection of questions matters more than attempting everything in order.

90-day plan for this section
  • Days 31–60: learn DI, Venn diagrams, data sufficiency, advanced probability and P&C.
  • Days 61–80: timed sets of 14 in 25 minutes. Practise choosing the easiest 8–9 first.
  • Days 81–90: full-section simulations inside full mocks.
Strategy and exam tactics
  • Start with data-set and puzzle questions, since one set can carry several questions.
  • For DI, estimate and approximate instead of exact calculation.
  • Data sufficiency: test each statement alone, then together, in that order.
  • Target 9–10 correct answers, which is a safe score. Do not chase all 14.
Common mistakes: Reading a full DI set before checking the questions · Over-calculating instead of approximating · Staying stuck on one puzzle

Learning order

Data Interpretation → Set Theory and Venn → Data Sufficiency → Puzzles → Advanced P&C → Advanced Probability → Partnership → Critical Reasoning

1 DI set, 1 puzzle set and 3 advanced questions daily. Weekly: 1 timed section of 14 Qs in 25 min.

  • Can I solve a 3-circle Venn problem with formulas?
  • Can I judge data sufficiency without calculating the full answer?
  • Can I spot the strengthening or weakening choice in critical reasoning?

Must-know rules

  • Use 64-bit integers (long long, long) when sums or products can be large.
  • Always test: empty input, one element, all equal, maximum size, negatives.
  • Solve the Easy problem fully in about 35 minutes before the Medium.

Common traps

  • Not reading constraints before choosing the approach.
  • Missing multiple test cases or a wrong input and output format.
  • Integer overflow and off-by-one errors.
  • Spending 60 minutes on the Easy problem.
  • Switching languages during preparation.

Advanced Coding: 90-day strategy

Day-by-day plan, exam tactics and score milestones for this section.

Advanced Coding2 problems · 90 min · Easy ~35 + Medium ~55

The section that decides Digital and Prime. Judged on hidden test cases, so edge cases matter.

90-day plan for this section
  • Days 1–30: one language, basics, loops, arrays, strings, math (80–100 Easy problems).
  • Days 31–60: sorting, searching, hashing, two pointers, recursion, stack, queue (Easy to Medium).
  • Days 61–90: Medium problems on DP, greedy, graphs basics. Solve 2 problems in 90 minutes, daily.
Strategy and exam tactics
  • Read constraints first to estimate the required time complexity (n up to 10^5 means no O(n²)).
  • Write a brute-force solution first so you have partial marks, then optimise.
  • Always test: empty input, one element, all equal elements, maximum size, negative numbers.
  • Solve the Easy problem completely in about 35 minutes before starting the Medium.
  • Match the input and output format exactly. Extra spaces or text can fail every test.
Common mistakes: Not handling multiple test cases · Integer overflow · Spending 60 minutes on the Easy problem

Learning order

Basics and patterns → Arrays → Strings → Math → Sorting and searching → Hashing → Two pointers → Recursion → Stack and queue → Greedy → DP → Graph and tree basics → Bit manipulation

Daily target

2 problems daily (1 Easy + 1 Easy/Medium). Weekly: 1 timed round of 2 problems in 90 min.

Self-check questions

  • Can I write input parsing for multiple test cases without errors?
  • Can I state the time complexity of my solution?
  • Can I list five edge cases before I submit?
  • Can I solve a standard DP problem (coin change, LIS) from scratch?

Rules to remember
